# E9 — UX-herwerking na de directie-review

**Phase:** 3 (parallel to E4/E5) · **Milestone:** none of its own — it repairs M2/M3 screens that already ship
**Goal:** Make the built screens usable by the teachers who will actually use them. Five change requests the project owner brought back from a session with directie on **2026-08-19**: cut the explanatory prose out of the default view, make planning *inside* a themaperiode possible and legible down to the week and the day, let a teacher navigate weeks the way they navigate a calendar, show coverage progress while they are creating it, and stop offering a teacher goals that belong to another class.
**Covers FR:** FR-6.2, FR-6.3, FR-7.2 (E9-03/04/05), FR-3.2 (E9-07), FR-9.1 (E9-06), NFR-usability (E9-01/02).
**Constitution:** [Art. II.3](../CONSTITUTION.md) (language), [Art. IV.1/IV.2](../CONSTITUTION.md) (AI advisory), [Art. V.1](../CONSTITUTION.md#article-v--coverage-must-be-provable-dekking) (dekking computed, never stored), [Art. IX.2](../CONSTITUTION.md#article-ix--core-data-model-functional) (klas/leeftijd scoping), [Art. IX.3](../CONSTITUTION.md#article-ix--core-data-model-functional) (planningsblok granularity), [Art. XII](../CONSTITUTION.md) (colour + label).
**UX & a11y:** every story here is UI work, so every story here **starts with the `frontend-design` skill** and is looked at in a real browser at desktop *and* ~390px (the working agreement in [`CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md)). WCAG 2.2 AA; colour never alone.

## Why this epic exists, and what it is not

The build passed its own gates. Every screen in it was tested, audited and — since E0-10 — actually opened in a browser. **What none of those gates measure is whether a teacher wants to read the screen**, and the directie review says they do not: there is so much explanatory text that the data it explains is hard to find.

**This epic is not a redesign.** The themaoverzicht is explicitly fine and stays. The design system (ADR-0017), the token palette, the one-structural-hue rule and the Dutch copy catalogue all stand. What changes is **how much is on screen at once**, and **what a teacher can do once they are on it**.

> **A correction to a reading that would be easy to make here.** CR2 looks like new scope and is not. **FR-6.2 already reads *"De leerkracht kan thema's **en activiteiten** verslepen (drag-and-drop) tussen periodes"***, and **FR-6.3 leaves the view levels explicitly undecided** (*"exacte niveaus ter beslissing"*). Activiteiten have never been placeable in time in this build — E3 placed thema's and stopped. So E9-03/04 **close FR-6.2's second noun and answer FR-6.3**, rather than adding anything the functional analysis did not ask for. Whoever audits this should hold it to that standard and not to a lower one.

## The architectural decision that carries the epic — read before touching E9-03

**An activiteit is placed on a `DateOnly`, not on a planningsblok, and "week" is a rendering grouping of dates rather than a tier.**

The tempting design is a third `Planningsblokniveau` (`Week`, or `Dag`). It is wrong, on three independent grounds:

1. **Art. IX.3 and ADR-0013 fix the planning grid at the ratified two-tier themaperiode/subthemaperiode pair, configurable behind the E3-05 seam.** A week is a calendar unit, which is the exact assumption the domain enum is guarded against gaining — `Planningsblokniveau` has a test whose whole job is to fail when a calendar unit appears in it. Widening it to satisfy a UI request would compile in the assumption Art. XIV still leaves open.
2. **`Themaplaatsing.BlokStart` keys on a derived boundary that moves when the school edits its vakanties** (ADR-0020 §3), which is why `isVervallen` exists. A concrete teaching day does not move when a vakantie is edited — it either stays a school day or becomes a closure. Keying an activiteit on a block boundary would inherit a staleness problem it does not have.
3. **The two answer different questions.** A themaperiode answers *"in which stretch of the year does this thema live?"*; an activiteit on a Tuesday answers *"what am I doing on Tuesday?"*. Collapsing them into one ordinal space is the "two views disagree about the same period" defect the E3-02/E3-06 review had to repair twice.

So: a new aggregate member `Activiteitplaatsing { ActiviteitId, JaarplanId, Datum: DateOnly, Status, Volgorde }`, and the week view derives its columns from `Schooljaar` + `Schoolsluiting` the same way `Planningsblok` already derives `AantalOpenDagen`. **A subthema is not placed at all** — it inherits its span from the activiteiten placed under it, so there is no second thing to keep in step. Recorded as **[ADR-0023](../docs/adr/0023-activiteit-day-placement.md)** (accepted 2026-08-19); supersede rather than rewrite if it turns out wrong.

- [~] **E9-01 — One "Uitleg tonen" switch, default off** — *CR1. Owner ruling 2026-08-19. **Mechanism built 2026-08-19**, and it works. **But it removes ~4% of the prose, not most of it** — read the finding below before promising the directie anything.*
  Move every instructional sentence in the UI behind a single switch in the app header, **default off**, persisted per user (`localStorage`, no server round trip and no new endpoint — this is a view preference, not school data).
  *Done when:* a teacher who has never touched the switch sees no instructional prose on any screen; turning it on restores every sentence in place; the choice survives a reload; and the switch itself is reachable by keyboard and labelled.
  > **The line between the two halves is the whole story, and it is not "long text vs short text".** In scope: sentences that teach the tool — the `*Uitleg` keys, the placeholders that explain a concept rather than an input, the per-row repetitions of something already said above the list. **Out of scope, and these stay unconditional whatever the switch says:** every error, every degrade, every withheld-figure explanation, every "this control is not available and here is why" (the E3-06 rule), and every consequence a teacher is about to commit to. A teacher must never lose the sentence that tells them what just went wrong, or that the dekkingscijfer is being withheld, because they turned off help they were reading as clutter.
  > **The measurement, so the story can be checked rather than argued about** (taken 2026-08-19): **113 keys of ≥110 chars, ~19 500 chars total** — 46 under `kalender`, 18 `parameters`, 15 `import`, 11 `dekking`, 7 `matching`, 7 `doelen`, 5 `themabeheer`, 2 `spine`, 1 each `binnenkort`/`ongekoppeld`. The longest is `parameters.uitleg` at 402 chars. **That list is the starting set, not the answer** — several of those 113 are degrade copy and must stay.
  > **Do not delete the strings.** They move behind a condition; `nl.json` keeps them (Art. II.3) and the catalogue guard keeps checking them. The owner chose the switch over deletion precisely so a training can turn help back on.
  > **Delivered: the mechanism.** `UitlegProvider` + `useUitleg` + an `<Uitleg>` wrapper, and one toggle at the right edge of the nav row. Default off, persisted in `localStorage`, read in the state initialiser so a teacher who switched help **on** does not watch the page render without it and reflow. State on three carriers, two of them not colour: `aria-pressed`, font weight, and **a check glyph that is present or absent**. 10 tests, including that an error survives the switch in both positions and that the sentence is **removed from the DOM** rather than hidden (a `sr-only` paragraph would fix the look and nothing for a screen-reader user). One key added: `uitleg.schakelaar`.
  > **A wrapper component rather than `{isAan && …}`, and that choice earned its keep immediately.** It makes each call site *declare* that its sentence is instructional, makes the in-scope set greppable (`rg "<Uitleg"`), and cannot be half-applied invisibly — a forgotten sentence still renders, which is the safe direction.
  > **⚠ THE FINDING, and it is the most important thing in this story: the switch is not the mechanism that fixes CR1.** Applied honestly it hides **716 characters of 19 490, about 4%**. The other ~96% is **not** instructional prose. It is degrade copy, withheld-figure explanations, E3-06 withheld-control statements and **consequence disclosures about destructive actions** — every one of which `Uitleg`'s own rule keeps unconditional, because a teacher must never lose the sentence saying what went wrong or what an action will discard. The `*Uitleg` key suffix turned out to be a poor proxy: `binnenkort.uitleg` is the E3-06 rule, `dekking.geenVanDezeSoortUitleg` is a withheld figure, `parameters.vervallenUitleg` is a degrade. **Only three keys qualified**: `parameters.uitleg` (402 chars, the longest string in the app), `parameters.momentenUitleg` and `import.uitleg`.
  > **The sharpest case, caught by an existing test rather than by reading.** `kalender.sleepUitleg` looks like pure instruction and its first two sentences are — but its third clause is *"Verplaats je een AI-voorstel, dan wordt het jouw eigen keuze en telt het vanaf dan mee voor de dekking"*, a consequence E4-01 put there deliberately. Wrapping it turned `promises the count-by-moving consequence only on the tier where moving works` red. **Reverted, with the reason recorded at the call site.** Splitting the string would be the better UX and was deliberately not done: it would reword a key three audit rounds have worked over, and this story was held to adding keys only while another session held the catalogue.
  > **What this means for the owner, and it needs saying rather than burying:** CR1's complaint is real and the switch does not answer it. **The volume is mostly copy that must stay, so the remaining work is structural, not a toggle** — see the new **E9-08**.
  > *Trap:* `frontend/src/i18n/catalogus.test.ts` holds the E5-03 conditional-copy case law. A sentence that becomes conditional on the switch is **still** bound by the E5-03 rule — it may assert only what its own render condition guarantees, and "the user asked for help" guarantees nothing new.

  > **⚠ `aantalOpenDagen` is NOT the number to print, and finding that out is most of this story.** Checked in the code on 2026-08-19 rather than assumed: **`Schooljaar.IsLesdag` counts weekends**, because it excludes only closures and *nothing in this model represents a weekend* — its own docstring says so and calls it an open question. So a 5-week period reports `aantalOpenDagen = 35`, and a screen that printed it would tell a teacher their autumn thema has **35 schooldagen**. The school's vakanties and vrije dagen *are* already subtracted; Saturdays and Sundays are not.
  > **Do not fix this by making `IsLesdag` skip weekends.** `beschikbareWeken = ceil(TelOpenDagen / 7.0)`, and that figure is the **sole definition of "te vol"** (owner ruling 2026-07-31, E3-09). Excluding weekends would turn a 5-week period into `ceil(25/7) = 4` weeks, so every nominal 5-week thema would become *te vol* in the period built for it. The weekend-counting is load-bearing **for the weeks arithmetic** and correct there.
  > **So this story adds a second, display-only count — teaching days excluding Saturday and Sunday — and must not feed it into any weeks figure.** Name it so the two cannot be confused at a call site (`TelOpenDagen` vs a new `TelLesdagenZonderWeekend`, or better names), and document on both why a codebase has two day counts. **The risk to watch is the E3-02 defect returning:** two callers counting days differently is exactly what `TelOpenDagen` was centralised to end. The difference is legitimate here only because the two answer different questions (how long is this block in weeks / how many days will I stand in front of this class), and that has to be written down where both live.
  > *Copy trap:* "dagen" alone invites the calendar reading. **"schooldagen"**, and no em dash (owner, 2026-07-29).
  > **Delivered: `Schooljaar.TelOpenWeekdagen` and `PlanningsblokWeergave.AantalOpenWeekdagen`**, mirrored on the frontend `Planningsblok`. Six domain tests, and the one that matters is the **separation**: it spells out that `ceil(25/7) = 4` while `ceil(35/7) = 5`, so the cost of ever substituting this count into the te-vol arithmetic is visible rather than implied.
  > **It lives in the domain, not in the mapper, and that was not a free choice.** `PlanningsblokWeergave`'s existing documentation warns in as many words that answering this with *"a second, weekend-aware definition living in that mapper"* is *"the drift this project keeps paying for"* — the same warning explains why the field was renamed from `AantalLesdagen` to `AantalOpenDagen` in the first place. So both counts are the schooljaar's, sit next to each other, and each says what it is for; the mapper only asks.
  > **It deliberately does not answer the open `IsLesdag` question.** That method still excludes only closures, and whether it *should* exclude weekends stays the school's to decide. This adds a second fact ("how many days will I stand in front of this class?") rather than changing the first ("how long is this block?"), so nothing depending on `IsLesdag` moved — which is what made it safe to build without a ruling.
  > **The field is required, not optional.** Optional would let a consumer forget it and render *"undefined schooldagen"*; the cost was 51 test fixtures, which is the right trade.
  > **⚠ Gate honesty, because the road to that green line was not straight.** Two earlier integration runs failed **27** tests each, spread across classes this change cannot touch (`AggregaatGroei`, `KlasEndpoints`, `LeerplandoelRegister`, `SchooljaarPersistentie`); one of those tests passes in isolation, and the clean run has **0** FAIL markers in fully retained output. The second failing run overlapped a second `dotnet test` against the same PostgreSQL instance, which is a sufficient explanation for it. **The first one is unexplained, because I piped its output through `grep` and destroyed the error text** — the diagnostic was gone before I knew I needed it. Recorded rather than smoothed over: a green suite reached after two unexplained reds is weaker evidence than one reached first time, and a reader deserves to know which this was. Probably related: **65 leaked `jp_test_` databases** on the local instance, up from the 26 **E7-14** recorded the same morning. Escalated in the groepschat as a live gate hazard rather than filed as debt.
  > *Open question this exposes, for the owner:* Flemish primary schools do not teach Wednesday afternoons. Whether a half day counts as a school day here is a school question, not a code one. **Do not model it without a ruling** — count whole days and say nothing about halves.

  > **Two things fell out of that, both in code this story did not set out to touch.** `useThemas` documented the *opposite* mechanism — *"removing a query that still has a mounted observer makes that observer fetch again immediately"* — which is half right: it refetches only once something **else** re-renders it, guaranteed in a delete because the same handler invalidates two lists.
